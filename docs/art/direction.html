DEAD WAX — art direction v1

two worlds, one scratch. if it looks like it took too long, redraw it faster.
FIDELITY LOCKED (2026-08-08): scratchy hand-drawn boil. Not pixel — the decision is settled (see art/style_compare.html for the four-way test and the mechanics table). House rules: characters are hand-drawn line art with 2–4 frame boil (freed 4f ≈ 10fps · Stuck 2f · machines 0f — boil IS the state readout). One line, two worlds: recolor, don't redraw — black ink on paper above the Scratch, white scratchboard line on black below. Sketchbook-rough (construction lines showing) is reserved for Soon only. A whisper of clean-vector polish is allowed on paper-world UI chrome and print, nowhere else. Pixel art is set aside; if it ever returns it means re-planning boil-is-state and the scratchboard world from scratch.
DENSITY TARGET (2026-08-08): dense & grungy, not minimal. Reference energy: Pizza Tower / Castle Crashers hand-drawn busyness, in our idiom. The placeholder line-art was reading too clean — the bar is art/cutaway.html. The recipe (the "detail language"): (1) thick tapering ink outlines — chunky black, heavier at joints, tapering to points (fill shapes, don't just stroke lines). (2) Interior hatching on every solid — parallel-line shading in the shadowed third of each form; cross-hatch the deep shadows. (3) Grime everywhere — flecks, water-streaks, wall-stains, scuffs; nothing is a clean flat fill. (4) Prop density / environmental storytelling — crates, tapes, bones, pipes, chains, lanterns, hanging flyers; every corner does a job. (5) Cutaway interiors — buildings and the world show their insides (a bed in the inn, the press in the print shop, rooms inside the walls). (6) Saturated flats UNDER the texture — rich base colors, then grime/hatch on top. Silhouette-first, always — density must never cost readability (critical now that combat is hard and tells must land in a frame). The two-world ink still holds: black ink + grime on paper above the Scratch, white scratchboard line + white grime out of black below.
THE TWO-TIER RULE (animation-cost honesty). Pizza-Tower density is glorious and frame-expensive — full hyper-detailed animation on every gameplay sprite is unaffordable at our scope. Resolution: Tier A — DETAILED (this density, few or no frames): key art, cutaways, character/boss portraits, cutscene stills, shop screens, the map. Tier B — READABLE (mid-detail + boil, built to move): in-combat gameplay sprites, where hyper-detail becomes visual noise at speed AND at hard-combat readability. Detail on Tier B comes from texture and thick silhouette, not frame-count. This is the standard prestige-2D split (gorgeous portraits, clean fighters). LOCKED 2026-08-08 — see the proof render art/tier_compare.html (the same Auditioner at 48px, dense vs readable, on a grimy floor). Density resolved UP, not down: the house look is dense-and-grungy, with a deliberate readability tier for anything the player fights.

01 · Key art study — the Scratch

The whole visual thesis in one frame: the played world is print on paper (aged label stock, misregistered inks, typography as architecture), the unplayed world is scratchboard (white line dug out of black wax, hatched, boiling). The Scratch is the only thing that belongs to both.

Print misregistration on paper; white line out of black below; pink is always noise being heard; the one gold fleck is her. Runtime plates instead of street numbers.

04 · Boil is state — live spec

Line boil isn't decoration here, it's diegesis: a soul's framerate. Freed people boil at 4 frames (~10fps). The Stuck boil at 2 — trapped between the same two drawings forever. The Changer doesn't boil at all, and it should be the most unsettling thing on screen because it's the cleanest.

Pink is semantic. Crackle pink appears only where sound is being heard right now — strikes, aggro ripples, your own noise. If a screenshot has pink in it, something is loud in it.

Gold is rationed. Etch gold belongs to the Engineer: the etching, her lathe, the Hound's tag, the last bar. Six appearances, game-wide. Budget enforced in review.

Paper never appears below the Scratch except: the liner-book UI, the Bootlegger's tape labels, and flyers that fell. Each one should feel like litter from a lost world.

White line weight = matter. Thick outline: solid wax. Thin + hatched: worn. Dotted: missing, unplayed elsewhere, or remembered.

07 · Reference kit — go hunting

SCRATCHBOARD (the below-Scratch look)

Study how real scratchboard artists build tone from white-line density — it's engraving in reverse, which is thematically free for us. Technique overviews: Wikipedia, Britannica, GNSI. Search: scratchboard texture · scratchboard illustration white line · clawboard black

LABEL / PRINT (the above-Scratch look)

Early phonograph label design is the Label district's entire architecture language — borders, lockups, all-type layouts: Edison cylinder guide, 78rpm label gallery. Search: edison gold moulded cylinder label · 78 rpm record label typography · dead wax runout etching macro · punk gig flyer xerox

MOTION / LINE (the register)

Your era, your people: David Firth's Salad Fingers for deadpan dread, Krinkels' Madness Combat for economy of violence, Dan Paladin for chunky readable silhouettes, and Klei's Don't Starve as the proof that scratchy-ink ships commercially. Search: newgrounds 2006 art style · boil animation 3 frames · don't starve concept art · flash animation 2005 aesthetic

ODD-DUCK BOARDS (trust me)

waveform embroidery (character hems) · vinyl crackle texture (noise VFX) · locked groove vinyl (gravestones) · phonograph tonearm patent drawing (B1's anatomy — patent linework is basically our style already)

How to board it

One board per stratum + one per character, 15–30 pins each, then cull to 9. A mood board you can't fit on one screen is a mood, not a board. Anti-references matter too: if a pin looks like Hollow Knight's filigree or anything expensive, it's off-model — this game's beauty is that it looks like it was made by one feral kid with a lightbox, at 2am, out of love.
