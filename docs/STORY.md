*The narrative doc. Grows act by act. Register law applies everywhere: the book carries the mania, the world plays it straight, and nothing funny touches a Rite. This file starts where the game does.*

# THE OPENING — "Side A, Track 1" (~4 minutes to player freedom)

## What the opening must do (and what it must not)

Must: teach movement with zero tutorial text · establish the mute kid and the book as his only voice · establish a world holding its breath · plant the guilt **without stating it** · aim the player downward.

Must not: show a title (the title card belongs to the Landing, end of Act 1) · play music (the score is *earned* — see the audio arc) · reveal the world's shape (the pullback belongs to the Drop) · confess (the word "mine" belongs to the Drop; see CANON PATCH below).

The opening is designed to *protect the two Act 1 reveals*. Every rejected alternate died for leaking one of them.

## The audio arc (the whole act, for context)

Act 1 has no music. It has: vinyl crackle (the room tone of a dead world), wind in worn grooves, and — rising through the act — **the stutter**: somewhere far below, the world is still trying to play itself, and keeps catching on the Scratch. A half-bar of the Voice, playing, catching, jumping back. The player will hear that half-bar maybe forty times before the Drop. It should be beautiful and *maddening* — an earworm with a wound in it. The first full, uninterrupted music in the game is the title-card moment at the Landing, below the Scratch, where the song was never played. The entire first act is musical foreplay: the game teaches you to *ache* for resolution before it ever explains that resolution is the whole plot.

## Beat by beat

**0. Studio jingle.** Black. One second of runout hiss, one soft pop. (That's the whole logo treatment. If we ever have a splash screen, it is a spinning runout groove and nothing else.)

**1. The stutter, in the dark.** Black screen, crackle bed. Far below, muffled: the half-bar plays — catches — jumps back. Three times, each a little closer, like something turning over in its sleep. On the third: cut to image.

**2. The Headshell, dawn-grey.** Wide shot. The Tonearm in silhouette: a great crane bowed over its cradle, asleep — and hanging in its worn grip, a small shape, hung up like a coat someone meant to come back for. Dust motes. The stutter reaches up here as a faint tremor; a thread of dust falls off the arm. Push in, slow. On the next stutter, in the dark of the little shape: **two pale dots open.** After decades, something below was finally loud enough to wake him.

**3. The first verb is wriggling.** Player input arrives while Skip is still *held*. A/D sways him in the grip — teaching movement before there is anywhere to move. The grip is old; he is small; three good sways and he **slips** — and drops with the game's first heavy THUD. Dust ring. Weight established forever. Below, the stutter answers the thud, one beat late — as if the world noticed. The arm does not wake. **The empty grip stays open-shaped on screen, and stays that way all game.** (It will notice he's gone. It will know exactly where he's going. It will be waiting at the bottom of the Overture — not garrisoned there: *arrived first.* The B1 fight is not a guard post. It's a catch, attempted early.)

**4. First steps, first page.** Walk left: the platform ends at the shaft mouth — fog, a dormant Spindle junction, no way down here. The descent is seen before it is possible: the game's oldest trick, played straight. Walk right, toward town, and the book slides in — a paper scrap, bottom corner, the game's first text:

> ok. inventory: one (1) me. one (1) scratch, world-sized.
> plan: go down. fix it. easy.
> write that down— i am. i just did.

**5. The Horn Plaza — the Hound.** The great horn, silent, monumental. Beneath it, the first living thing in the game: the Hound, one ear to the ground. It lifts its head. Comes over. Sniffs. One tail wag — two frames, per the boil law. And here the game teaches its most important mechanic wordlessly: **stand still near the Hound, and Skip pets it.** No prompt, no button. Stillness is how you touch things gently in this game — the same stillness that will later be the stealth verb, the listening verb, the mercy verb. The player learns LIFT's heart before they learn LIFT.

> the dog remembered me.
> dogs don't keep score. write that down too.

**6. The High Street — kind avoidance.** Three vignettes on one walk, no cutscenes, all skippable at a run:
- **Matron 33⅓** sweeps her step. Sees him. Stops. Her six worn pleasantries visibly fail her — none of them fit this. She settles on: *"...mind the step, love."* And goes in.
  > she used to have a phrase for me.
  > it wore out.
- **The Typesetter**, through his window, setting tomorrow's poster for no one. Looks up. Nods, once. Back to work.
- **The Postie**, mid-street, mid-delivery, one letter forever almost through one slot. The player's first Stuck — no explanation, no lore popup. Just a man who has been Almost There for decades.
  > (the postie is still Almost There. don't stare. it's rude.)

Nobody says his name. Nobody says *any* name. A first-time player reads a shy, gentle town. The truth — that every one of these people knows exactly what he did and has decided to be kind anyway — is not stated here, or anywhere in Act 1. It is stated at the Drop, by the book, in three words, and the whole town retroactively breaks your heart.

**7. The overlook — the pull.** End of the plaza: a rail, and past it the shaft proper, fog-filled. The stutter comes again — loudest yet, directly below. The camera tips downward one degree, one beat — a taste, not a reveal — and Skip's point-tip lights one pink tick: the world's noise, touching him for the first time. The book, last page of the opening:

> listen to it down there. still trying.
> nobody's going to go fix that.
>
> nobody else.

Player freedom. Signs route to the Practice Room. The opening never said the word Scratch out loud, never played a note of music, never showed the world's shape, and never confessed. Four minutes; all four debts planted.

## CANON PATCH (applied to CAST.md, same day)

The book's first page previously read *"one (1) scratch, world-sized, **mine**."* — that single word spoils the Drop. The confession now lives in exactly one place: the reveal, where the book writes *"it's a record. this is the scratch. **it's mine.**"* Page one drops "mine." Additionally, the Tonearm's framing is sharpened: it doesn't garrison the crossing, it *arrives first* — the B1 fight is a parent's catch, attempted one act too late.

## Rejected openings (kept for the record)

- **The Last Playback (flashback cold open):** play the 60 seconds before the skip — the world bright and loud, the dust drift ahead, smash cut. Rejected as an opener: it confesses in the first minute and spends the flashback card. **Salvaged:** it becomes the centerpiece SET memory in the Whisper Strata — the player *plays the skip* mid-game, hands on the wheel, after they already love the world it broke. Far crueler there. Far better.
- **The Ascent Shot (camera tours the cylinder bottom-to-top before finding Skip):** gorgeous, and it leaks the map-shape reveal that belongs to the Drop. Dead on arrival.

# THE THRESHOLD — "that sinking feeling" (Act 3 open)

*The technique is the Amber assembly (House, "Wilson's Heart"): every piece of evidence is shown long before the reveal; the reveal is only the moment the pieces re-order — and the player must arrive one beat before the text confirms it. Nothing here is new information. That's the law of this scene.*

## The truth underneath

**"Soon" is not a name. It is a title** — the world's word for whoever holds the ending and cannot let it end. The current holder is the mark. The next holder is whoever carries the ending to the Last Bar and hesitates. Skip has been collecting the Last Bar in fragments all game. He is carrying the ending down the world. The seat is waiting.

## The planted evidence (all pre-shown, none explained)

1. **The graffiti.** "soon." on the walls from early Act 2 — read as the villain signing territory. *The walls aren't signed. They're addressed.*
2. **The Welcome.** It caught him mid-fall, held him, examined him, thanked him. Read as seduction. It was *recognition* — what you do when your successor arrives.
3. **The second sign.** Under NOW SERVING: ∞, smaller, present from the first Green Room visit: **NEXT: ________**. Set dressing, until it isn't.
4. **The naming canon itself.** Skip has no runtime, no formal name — the blank between tracks. Planted in Act 1 dialogue and the book. *The ticket is blank because his name is.*
5. **The book's tic.** From the first "soon." sighting, the liner-book compulsively copies the graffiti into its margins — tiny, repeated, easy to dismiss as texture. It's the montage, pre-loading itself.

## The scene (Green Room threshold, point of no return)

Skip walks the last corridor of the Green Room. The waiting ones — sketched half-figures holding tickets — **stand as he passes.** One by one. Not menacing. *Reverent.* The next act just walked in.

The crackle stops. Not the music — the **crackle**. (Hush's dropout kills music and never touches crackle; crackle is the room tone of reality itself. Its total absence happens exactly once in the game: here, standing inside the held breath. Three full seconds.)

The book flips itself backward — page after page of margin-copied *soon. soon. soon.* — the assembly, played at reading speed. Then, in the small neat hand it saves for Rites:

> the walls aren't signed. they're addressed.

*(beat)*

> the ticket's blank because my name is.

*(beat)*

> get up. keep walking.
> we are not taking the seat.

Camera holds on the ticket booth as he passes it. NEXT: ________. No sting. Crackle resumes like breath.

## The payoff — THE LAST BAR (final input of the game)

At the etching, at the end of the Run: the strike prompt. The player presses strike. **Nothing happens.** Once. And the book auto-writes, unbidden, in Skip's own hand:

> soon

The player watches their character's handwriting produce the villain's word. Then the truth of the final input reveals itself: **the Last Bar is not a press. You HOLD strike — the note swells for as long as you hold — and the game will let you be Soon for as long as you choose. The last input of DEAD WAX is a release.**

- **Rite path:** the player breaks their own hold with the cue — the hand-fall gesture learned freeing Soon. You free the villain with that gesture, then you free yourself with it. The mechanical rhyme is the ending's whole argument.
- **Force path:** the cue was never learned. After ten held seconds the world takes the note from you — the release happens *to* Skip, not *by* him — and the epilogue's last page is written in a shakier hand.
- Some players will release in two seconds. Some will hold for five minutes. Both are playing the game correctly. The hold is the temptation made playable; the button teaches the thesis: *let it end.*

## Ripples (no retcons required)

- **Hush's fury re-reads a third time.** "they are not waiting. they are dying slowly. finish or free them." — he knew what stalling makes of someone. His overprotection of Skip was never only about the wax. He has watched one sibling take the seat's logic already; he will not watch the little one sit down.
- **The Engineer's etching re-reads.** "If you are reading this, you skipped. Good. It means you kept playing." — *kept playing* is the antidote. Motion versus hold. She wrote the counter-spell into her last line.
- **Soon's Rite re-reads.** Giving Soon its cue is not just mercy — it is rehearsal. The player cannot save themselves at the Last Bar with a gesture they never learned to give away.

# CUTSCENES — Act 1 & the Welcome (full scripts)

## Cutscene philosophy (binding)

Almost nothing in DEAD WAX takes the controller away. Scenes stage themselves *around* a player who can still walk; the camera does the acting; text arrives via the book, which never pauses the world. True control-loss is rationed to three moments in the whole game — the Drop's pullback (2 seconds), the Welcome's hold (because loss of control IS the scene), and the eaten input at the Last Bar (one press). Every other "cutscene" below is playable staging. Skippable on repeat playthroughs, never on the first — the game is 3 hours; we can ask for four minutes of trust.

## SCENE: ADDIE'S DOORWAY — the first mercy

*Overture, ~3 minutes below the Label. The whole Rite grammar taught in ninety seconds, in a doorway, with a lullaby.*

**Approach.** Her loop is audible a full room away — the game's first Stuck voice. A lullaby that keeps almost-ending: *"...and the night will hold you tigh—"* — catch — *"...and the night will hold you tigh—"*. The player rounds the corner: ADDIE in her doorway, 2-frame boil, arms cradling a bundle that isn't there.

> the singing lady is stuck on the goodnight.
> she's been saying it for longer than i've been down.
> nobody ever got to sleep.

**The bar.** Standing inside her loop = a gentle push-back, no damage. (Stuck are never enemies; the world just says *excuse you.*)

> she didn't see me. she can't see anything.
> she's all one moment.

**The thread.** Her doorway faces up-shaft; scratched into the doorframe, small, old: a child's height-marks, and beside the top one — a locked-groove glyph and an arrow pointing UP. The trail leads *backward*, to the Locked-Groove Yard above the Label: the game's first taught backtrack. At the Yard, one groove hums when approached — SET into it: **a child's answering hum, slightly flat, then a giggle, then: "again."** The flat little hum is the next bar. It was always sung back wrong, and that was the whole point of it.

*(book, small neat hand — the line's origin:)*

> i know your next bar. hold still.
> i learned it for you.

**The mercy.** Back at the doorway: SET at the loop's edge. Skip plays the flat hum into the gap — and the lullaby lands: *"...and the night will hold you tight."* Full stop. The first completed sentence in the Overture. **Her boil jumps from 2 frames to 4 on screen** — the framerate law made visible; this is the permanent VFX of every mercy in the game. She sags. Blinks. Looks down at the bundle-shape her arms still make, and lets them fall, slowly, empty. Then she looks at Skip — the first eye contact any living thing has given him.

> ADDIE: ...oh.
> ADDIE: everyone calls me Addie. called. everyone called me Addie.

And then the line the whole scene exists for. She takes in the hood, the tape-wrapped point, the small iron kid who just finished her song — and she doesn't ask what he did. She says:

> ADDIE: ...you have her hands.

*(book — after, walking away, letters unsteady:)*

> ok. crying is loud. hood up.
> hood UP.

**After.** She putters the Overture permanently. Waters things that aren't plants. The Hound visits her on its rounds. If Skip stands still near her, she pats the hood, twice, like tucking in a collar.

## SCENE: THE ARM WAKES — B1 pre-fight

*Trigger: Skip descends the last Overture stair toward the Arm arena.*

**Above.** A groan of old metal rolls down the shaft. Every dust mote on screen shivers. Cut — the one brief hard cut of Act 1 — to the Headshell, far above: **the empty grip closes on nothing.** Beat. The Tonearm rises off its cradle for the first time in decades. Below it, the town: Matron on her step, the Typesetter in his window, the Hound by the horn — all looking up. Nobody moves to stop it. Matron, quietly, to the machine as it goes:

> MATRON 33⅓: bring him back whole, you old hinge.

*(The town's first acknowledgment, out loud, that they know exactly where he went.)*

**The descent.** Back on Skip, playable. The arm comes down the *outside* of the shaft — glimpsed through gallery windows as it passes, too big for the rooms, a shadow with joints. As it passes the player's position the screen trembles once.

> that sound is for me.

**The arena.** It's waiting above the sealed Drop — arrived first, the way it always meant to. It does not attack. It raises its cradle-hand and points, once, UP. *Go home.* And then it waits — **the parent never swings first.** The B1 fight begins only when the player strikes. That is a design law: its first grab comes after your first blow, every time, so every player carries their share of this fight.

**Resolution — FORCE.** The grip cracks; the arm slumps against the shaft wall. It does not rage. It drags itself to the edge of the opening seal and *watches him fall* — "HELD" sampling slower, and slower, until it's just her voice, stretched into a groan. The last thing visible topside:

> don't look up. don't look up.

**Resolution — RITE.** The knowledge lives in the Yard: the locked groove of the night she hung Skip in its grip. SET plays her voice — the full charge, the second half of which the arm never let itself hear:

> THE ENGINEER *(played)*: hold him even when he's heavy. especially then.
> THE ENGINEER *(played)*: ...and when he's ready, love — you set him down.

Play the second line mid-fight, in the gap after a missed grab. The arm stills. The grip opens — by choice, for the first time. And it performs its whole purpose, the job after the job: it lifts Skip, carries him over the seal, and **lowers him through the Scratch itself** — the gentlest drop in the game, the act break as a parent's hands. As Skip descends past the wound, the last thing visible topside is the arm leaning over the edge, grip still open — the same open shape from the opening's first shot, now meaning the opposite thing.

## SCENE: THE DROP — shot by shot

*The reveal. Timings assume 60fps; the whole sequence is ~20 seconds and unskippable exactly once.*

- **S1 — 0:00.** Release: the arm lowers him (Rite) or Skip steps past the broken grip and jumps (Force). Camera tight.
- **S2 — 0:02.** Contact lost. Freefall through the gouge — raw rock-wax cross-section, the Scratch seen from *inside*. Wind, for the first time in the game, loud. Crackle rising.
- **S3 — 0:04.** **THE PULLBACK.** Camera zooms out 4× over two seconds — the only camera move of its kind so far. The walls curve away. The groove-wall texture resolves. The stutter is *close* now, doppler-warping as he falls past its source-depth. *(Control is gone for exactly these two seconds.)*
- **S4 — 0:06.** The book tumbles open beside him, pages riffling, and sketches the helix in three strokes. Writes: **"it's a record."** — beat — **"this is the scratch."**
- **S5 — 0:08.** **"it's mine."** One breath of wind-only quiet. (Not crackle-silence — that is reserved for the Threshold. The world keeps crackling. Only the *book* goes still.)
- **S6 — 0:09.** Control returns, promptless, mid-fall. Nearly every player panic-strikes — and for the first time, **the air answers.** The jet fires pink and enormous; the arc bends; the fall becomes steerable. No fail state, fog layers to fall through, the reveal is *played*: the double jump arrives as the world changing.
- **S7 — 0:15.** Landing — soft, on wax burnished mirror-flat, a bloom of dust. A beat of stillness before the player moves.
    > ...who polished the floor?
    *(Hush assist #1, planted in five words.)*
- **S8 — 0:18.** **TITLE CARD: DEAD WAX.** And the first uninterrupted music in the game — the stutter's half-bar, heard forty times, finally *continuing into its next phrase.* The earworm resolves; the score enters; chills by construction, not by volume. Hold three seconds. Card out. The Landing. Act 2.

## SCENE: THE WELCOME — Soon's entrance

*Undersong gate, mid-Act 2. The graffiti density has been rising for an hour. The scene's spine is a mechanical rhyme: the same wriggle input from the opening's grip, played back with the meaning inverted.*

**The hold.** Skip drops through the gate shaft — and the fall *stops.* Not the music (Hush's sign), not the crackle (the Threshold's) — the *fall*. Skip hangs six feet above the floor, toes down, gently rotating. **Input stays live:** A/D sways him in the hold, exactly like the opening's grip. Every player tries it. It doesn't work. The last time something held him like this, it loved him. The controller teaches the difference.

**The arrival.** Nothing enters. The background *resolves* — construction lines in the wall hatching un-camouflage, gather, and stand: it was in the drawing the whole time. (Retroactive shiver: every hatched background in the act now might have been partly it. This is also permission for the art team to hide its linework anywhere.) The lidded eye opens — the only finished thing on it.

**The audience.** It circles him slowly. It speaks — straight register, never completing a sentence:

> SOON: oh— look at you— look at what you—
> SOON: the little father— the giver of forever—
> SOON: do you know what you made? nothing can be lost now— nothing ends— nothing has to—
> SOON: you must be so tired— all that down, down, down— and for what— an ending—
> SOON: endings are where songs give up— but you didn't give up— you *stopped*— stopping is mercy— stopping is—
> SOON: stay— be held— be almost— with me—
> SOON: soon—

**The release.** It sets him down. Gently. And then the detail the whole scene is built to deliver: it reaches out and **brushes the dust off him** — the thing that ended the world, groomed off his shoulders like lint, tenderly, by the thing that profits from the ending's absence. Then:

> SOON: thank you— for the time— for all this time—

— and the lines de-resolve into the wall hatching. Gone. Every "soon." in the room is freshly re-chalked; fine chalk dust still falling. It was just here. It is always just here.

*(book — first shaky, one line crossed out, then neat:)*

> ~~it held me like~~
> it said thank you.
> nobody says thank you.
> keep moving.

# THE LAST PLAYBACK — the playable memory (Whisper Strata)

*The flashback we refused to open the game with, placed where it cuts deepest: mid-Act 2, after the player loves the world, findable in the Whisper Strata's lowest chamber. The game's only full-color, full-music sequence — because it's the only scene set while the world was alive.*

**Finding it.** The deepest listening spot in the Strata sits apart from the others, and the wax around it is *worn almost through* — the tell of a groove played hundreds of times. The book, before Skip sets:

> the groove here is worn nearly to the other side.
> someone plays this one. over and over and over.
> who would do that to themse—
>
> oh.

*(Unstated, unmissable: the wear is HUSH's. The First Stylus has spent decades down here, replaying his little brother's worst minute, trying to steer it different. "footwork. the deep is worse." was never about combat.)*

**The memory.** SET — and the world blooms. Color. MUSIC — the Voice, alive, mid-verse, glorious. The player controls young Skip inside the groove on the night: hopping (of course), the lantern-bright walls, her voice somewhere above humming along with her own recording. For forty seconds the game is simply *happy*, and the player gets to be good at it — the movement sings, the world answers, this is what playing used to mean.

**The rule of the memory: the groove goes where it went.** The dust drift is ahead, and the player — who knows — will try to steer around it. The memory *resists revision*: swerve, and the line drifts back; brake, and the tempo carries you; jump, and the groove is still under you when you land. The book, present-tense, overlaid small in the corner:

> it's pressed. you can't unpress it.
> play it anyway.

**The moment.** The dust arrives where the dust was. The skip fires whether or not the player presses anything — *there is no input for the worst moment of your life; it happens to you* — smash-cut to black, one heartbeat of the stutter, and then Skip is kneeling at the listening spot in the cold violet present, point still set, not moving.

> i tried to steer it different.
>
> everyone who plays this tries to steer it different.

**After.** The worn groove is fractionally deeper now. Skip's replay joined all of his. If the player returns and sets again, the memory replays in full — the game never gates it — but the book writes only one line on repeat visits: *"i know. me too."*

# THE CUE — how the release is learned (Whisper Strata)

*The gesture that frees Soon, rehearsed here, performed twice. Found in her practice sessions — a separate listening cluster: the Engineer, alone, rehearsing the ending she was writing.*

**What Skip hears.** She practiced conducting the Last Bar with a metronome — and the metronome was Tick ("i was her metronome. i was a GOOD metronome"). Take after take, across several listens:

> THE ENGINEER *(played)*: again. one — two — three — four — *(the held breath; the long note; a soft sound of fabric falling)* — ...and *there.*
> THE ENGINEER *(played)*: no. held it too long. it gets heavy if you hold it too long, doesn't it. again.
> THE ENGINEER *(played)*: one — two — three — four — *(hold)* — ...and there. *(a small laugh)* there. that's all it is, in the end. you count someone in, you hold them while it lasts, and then — there.

**The design freight in one word:** *"there."* is her release word — the word her hand fell on. It is the word Soon has been starving for since she died: the cue that never came. It is the first word she ever said to Skip (see the Cradle-Lathe listen, Act 3). And it is the only complete sentence Soon will ever speak. One syllable, threaded through the entire endgame — planted here, in her voice, laughing.

**The mechanics.** The cue is not a refrain and has no tab. It is a *gesture*: **hold strike — and release.** Learned by ear here, performed for the first time at Soon's Rite, performed for the last time at the Last Bar. The final input of the game is rehearsed on the villain before it is asked of the player. Mercy as practice for letting go — the design and the thesis are the same sentence.

# ACT 3 — THE COMPLETE SCRIPT

## The Green Room walk

The corridor stretches. Wallpaper, coat hooks, a carpet runner going the exact wrong kind of soft underfoot. Ticket stubs drift like leaves. The waiting ones line both walls in chairs — sketched half-figures, tickets in their laps — and here is where the congregation system pays its debt: **every named Stuck the player skipped is sitting in this room.** Walt, mid-spin even seated, if you never taught him the step. The Postie with his letter, if the slot never completed. Each under a small chalk *"soon."* They can be freed here — Act 3 makes room for late mercy — but the walk makes you *see* the backlog first.

And if the player freed everyone: **rows of empty chairs.** The book, small and neat:

> everyone i helped isn't here.

> that's what helping is. it's making this room emptier.

Then the ticket booth. NOW SERVING: ∞. NEXT: ________. The waiting ones stand, one by one, reverent. The crackle stops. **The Threshold scene plays as scripted.** *"get up. keep walking. we are not taking the seat."*

## The Locked Grooves

The never-played graves. Every chalked name is in the same unfamiliar hand — careful, block-lettered, patient. One note, wedged:

> i chalked their names. someone had to.

*(HUSH has been the deep's sexton the whole time. He couldn't play them and wouldn't erase them, so he did the third thing: he made sure they were written down.)*

## THE RUNOUT — B5, both paths

**The arena.** The pooled Unheard, and Soon above them, baton raised — conducting the congregation the audit left it. A thorough player faces a thin choir and a thinner king; a racer faces a wall of sound with hands.

**The dropout comes.** Music cuts mid-bar — and for the first time in the game, the silence feels like backup. **HUSH arrives** (Rites path), takes the left flank without a word, burnisher up. Or — raced path — he stands *with* the Unheard, veil down, and the book writes the saddest line of that route: *"he thinks he's protecting them from me. maybe he is."*

**Force finish.** The hold is broken by damage. Soon dies the way it always feared: mid-almost. Its last line stays unfinished — *"but i was going to—"* — and the sentence just stops. Nothing completes it. That silence follows the player into the shakier-handed epilogue.

**The Rite: the cue, performed.** In the fight's held moment — Soon rearing to its full sketched height, one bar of total stillness — the player gives it: **hold strike... and release.** Her gesture. The hand falls at last, decades late, in her student's hand. Soon holds. Looks. And releases — the note it rehearsed for generations, one single sustained tone, the most beautiful sound in the game, rising as the construction lines finally, willingly, resolve: for one moment Soon is *finished* — inked, complete, gorgeous — and then gone, bowing, and it speaks its whole sentence:

> SOON: *There.*

## THE RUN — the world plays through

Hush goes to the wound. (He has the whole game's climb to make and the scene gives him ten seconds of it — the last we see of him before the epilogue is a small grey figure ascending, burnisher over his shoulder like a workman going to a job.) At the Scratch, off-screen, the erasure that spent a lifetime being wrong is finally applied to the right thing.

And the world — freed at the seam — **plays.** The stutter's half-bar, heard a hundred times, catches... and *continues.* The playhead sweeps down from the Scratch through everything: and the game's own map screen becomes the finale — **the atlas lights, cell by cell, in playback order**, the ant-farm becoming a waveform, every room the player learned flaring as its passage sounds. Every freed soul sings their bar as the playhead passes: Addie's lullaby lands its last note in the cascade; Walt's waltz turns once; Vess and the Duet hand off, at last, in tempo. As the wave crosses the Horn Plaza, **the great horn sounds** — and threaded under the whole descending chorus, faint and unmistakable, one dog, howling along. The Hound's entire wait, paid in a single held note.

The wave arrives at Dead Wax. The Unheard rise *into* it — played at last, each syllable taken up into the song as it passes — us too, us too, *yes, you too, all of you* — and the music gathers at the etching, and waits.

## EPILOGUE — the good kind of quiet

The Label. Dawn-grey, again — the same shot that opened the game. No stutter. The first morning in decades without it, and the silence is *different*: not a held breath. A finished song.

Small staging, no text walls: Matron sweeps her step and, finding no one to say it to, says "back before dark" to the street itself, warmly, off-rotation forever now. The Typesetter's window: the poster frame finally says something new — set in caps, as promised. Addie waters her not-plants. The Hound trots its full route — Addie's doorway, the Yard, the Plaza — and sits, at last, facing the horn *without* an ear to the ground. Off-duty. **If the Rite path was taken: the Tonearm stands at its cradle, and as Skip crosses the plaza it lowers its open grip — an offer, not a catch — and Skip climbs in, and it lifts him to the Headshell, and holds him, and that is the final image: held, home, by choice on both sides.** The last book page, neat, tiny:

> it's quiet now.
> the good kind.

Credits: the tracklist. Every soul, formal name and worn name where one was earned, in playback order — including any the player's book quietly named. The last line of the credits is reserved for the true-name reveal *(placeholder honored; the drawer stays shut until Gabe opens it)*.

**Force-path deltas:** the arm is absent; its cradle empty; Skip climbs to the Headshell alone and sits in the open grip's shape without the grip. The book's last page is the same two lines, in a hand that isn't quite steady. The credits play. Fewer worn names appear. The song still ended. It cost more.
