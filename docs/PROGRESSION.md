## The one-line identity

**Power is loudness, and loudness is the thing that hunts you.** Every scrap of
strength in this game is a sound you took off something you killed, and carrying
it makes you easier to hear. Growth is Faustian by construction, not by flavour
text: the build that hits hardest is the build that can least afford to be quiet.

## The three forks, locked

| Fork | Choice | What it commits us to |
|---|---|---|
| **Depth of growth** | **Full RPG growth** | Not a handful of upgrades — a real stat, real slots, real levelling. Enough system that a build is a build. |
| **Cost of growth** | **Faustian** | Farming is possible and it costs something thematic every time, in-fiction and in-mechanics. No free strength. |
| **Drop model** | **Skill-scaled odds + soft pity** | How well you took the kill sets the odds. A visible floor guarantees you get there eventually. No pure RNG walls, no pure guarantees. |

## What a Cut is

A **CUT** is the shatter's word-splatter *caught clean and whole* instead of
spilled. When you finish something well, the words it was mid-saying freeze in
the air, ring **pink** — heard — and press to wax. You collect the last thing it
ever said.

That is the whole fiction of the loot system, and it is deliberately a little
awful: **the reward for killing something is a recording of its final sentence.**
Winning already feels a little sad (`COMBAT_FEEL.md`); the Cut is where that
sadness becomes an inventory.

## The three homes

A Cut is not a passive stat stick. It goes somewhere, and where it goes decides
what it does. Slotting a Cut anywhere means the same thing: *a burst of whatever
is pressed there plays.*

- **THE POINT** — active. Plays on command. Costs **crackle**, not mana: power
  has a volume, and spending it announces you to the room. The noise economy
  (`ENEMIES.md`, law 2) is the resource bar; there is no second one.
- **THE SLEEVE** — passive, always looping. Matron 33⅓'s sleeve-patches are the
  fitting: a **locked groove made literal**, the world's saddest fate turned into
  your armour. What's pressed there repeats forever, which is exactly what
  happened to everyone you took it from.
- **THE PARRY** — the rarest tier. A Cut here fires on a rung-back. This slot
  touches the heart of the game (`COMBAT_FEEL.md`: the parry is the only
  pure-joy action) and must be earned, never sold.

## The law that protects the spine

**Traversal is NEVER a Cut.** Gather, Rest, Jump-Cut and every other movement
grant stay **Rites**: guaranteed, story-placed, never RNG, never farmable. The
metroidvania is a designed lock-and-key game and it stays one.

RNG only ever buys **optional expression**. It must never gate progress, and a
player with terrible luck must be able to finish the game having missed nothing
but flourishes. This is the same promise `PUZZLES.md` makes — *no mandatory
grinding* — applied to loot.

## GAIN — the growth stat

One stat, **GAIN**, raised in roughly **12 notches**, fed by **shine** (minted by
polishing, per `PITCH.md`) and spent at **the Bootlegger**.

Priority order for what a notch buys, highest first:

1. **RING** — resonance. How fast you build toward a shatter.
2. **BODY** — HP and crackle capacity.
3. **BREADTH** — slots. More Cuts carried at once.
4. **BITE** — raw damage, and **capped**.

The order is the argument: **growth rings THROUGH the parry, never around it.**
A maxed player is faster to resonance and roomier in slots; they are not a player
who no longer needs to time anything. Bite is capped precisely so that no amount
of grinding converts into "I can ignore the tells now."

### The coupling that makes it Faustian

**GAIN raises your crackle floor.** The stronger you are, the louder you idle.
Strong and quiet become *opposed builds* — a stealth run is a low-GAIN run, and a
maxed run cannot use the Hood the way a fresh one can. This is the Faustian
bargain expressed mechanically instead of narratively, and it's the single most
important number in the system.

The deep scales alongside it, so the walls stay walls.

## Levelling by duplicates

Cuts level by **duplicates**: a second copy of a Cut you already own upgrades it
rather than cluttering the book. Grinding one lane keeps paying, so a player who
picks a lane is never punished for depth — and the payoff curve is theirs to
choose. **OPEN:** how many duplicates per level, and whether levels cap.

## The two valves

The system has two pressure valves, and they mirror each other exactly:

| | **MERCY** | **THE PRESS** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Free the thing instead of killing it | Take the Cut |
| What it costs | **Time** — SET-play, defenceless | **The audit, and your silence** |
| What it gives | Nothing you can carry | Power, and volume |

**Grinding is the sad way to win.** It is opt-in overlevelling, not the spine —
the balance spine is a no-grind skill run, and every encounter is tuned against
that. A player who never presses a single Cut must be able to finish the game.

And the audit is real: **B5 counts every soul you took.** `CAST.md` already makes
the final boss's difficulty into your kindness, audited — everything you played,
freed and polished is subtracted from its body. The Cut system puts a thumb on
the other side of that scale. Farming people makes the last fight harder, and the
game never says so out loud.

## What may be farmed: only the soulless

Farming is restricted, in fiction, to things that were never anyone:

- **SKIPPERS** — the wound's static (`ENEMIES.md` #4: *"they're not people,
  they're the wound's static"*). No grief, no Rite, no mercy. The intended
  starter lane.
- **EARWORMS** — parasites, not people. `ENEMIES.md` #6 already calls the Chorus
  *"a farmable stealth lane on the way back through"* once REST silences them.
- **BLANK ECHOES** — **a new ninth enemy type**, Dead Wax only, in the
  `locked_grooves`. 0-frame boil, no boil at all: sound that outlived whoever
  made it. Distinct from the Pressed, who are people who stopped; a Blank Echo
  was never a person. Farmable without cost. **This type is not yet in
  `ENEMIES.md` and needs a full entry there.**

**People-cuts are the richest and rarest** — and audited. That asymmetry is the
whole moral design: the best loot in the game is the loot you should not take.

## Skill is luck

How you finish decides what you get.

- A **clean, on-beat, rung-back take** — the shatter arriving off a parry, in the
  pocket — gives the best odds and the cleanest catch.
- An **ugly off-beat chip kill** gives muddy, low-tier results.

The word-splatter is the tell: caught clean, it freezes whole; chipped down, it
smears. You can see how good your take was before you see what dropped.

**SOFT PITY:** the book keeps a per-type margin tally. Near misses accumulate a
**visible floor** that eventually guarantees a Cut, then resets on drop. Bad luck
is survivable; the floor is legible, so nobody is farming in the dark.

## The rarity ladder

| Tier | Name | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Repress** | Common. The same sentence everyone has. |
| 2 | **White Label** | Uncommon. |
| 3 | **Test Pressing** | Rare. |
| 4 | **Dubplate** | Very rare. |
| 5 | **One-of-One** | **Bosses only.** Guaranteed, marquee, unrepeatable. |

Bosses always drop their One-of-One, so a boss is never a slot-machine. Hush
duels may drop **rival uniques** — the one place a repeatable fight yields
something singular.

## The Catalogue

The **CATALOGUE** is the liner-book's **B-side**: a discography of every phrase
you have caught. It is the deliberate mirror of the credits tracklist —

> **souls freed** on one side, **sounds taken** on the other.

Two lists, same book, and the player assembles both without ever being asked
which one they were building. **OPEN:** whether the Catalogue is readable before
the ending, and whether it is ever remarked on.

## Readability

Per the two-tier art split (locked 2026-08-08):

- **The drop is Tier B** — readable at gameplay distance: a pink freeze, a
  tier-coloured label ring, a clean-take chime. You know instantly that something
  dropped and roughly how good it was.
- **The trophy render is Tier A** — full density, in the book and on the press
  screen. That is where the detail lives.

## Scope and milestone plan

- **~25–30 Cuts total.** Curated. **None filler** — every Cut is a written line
  from a specific thing, and a Cut that isn't worth writing isn't worth pressing.
- **Prove the loop on ONE lane first** — the Skippers. If pressing Skippers is
  not fun on its own, more content will not save it.
- **This is M3.** It comes *after* the M2 combat slice. Do not build the Cut
  crate until the Auditioner and the Tonearm feel right (`COMBAT_FEEL.md` scope
  flag), because the drop is a reward for a fight that does not yet feel like
  anything.

**M3 needs, in build order:** the GAIN stat and shine as a real currency → the
Blank Echo enemy → the take-quality grade on a kill → the Cut press-screen → the
three slot homes → the Catalogue.
