# DEAD WAX — Cast (v2, 2026-08-08)

*Round 6 integrated: the protagonist is SKIP (working name — true name reserved for a late reveal), the world calls the role THE PLAYER, and nobody in-game ever says "needle." Every character is load-bearing: a grief that is a loop, a Rite that is its next bar, or a system they carry. Voice samples included — the register is half the design.*

**Roster at a glance:** 1 player character · 1 posthumous mother · 1 rival · 1 big bad · 5 bosses · 4 friendly NPCs · 4 named Stuck (micro-Rites) · 8 enemy types · 1 dog. Small casts mourn harder; this is the ceiling, not the floor to build past.

---

## SKIP — you. *(working name — the true name is a late-game reveal)*

The Engineer's stylus, given legs: mall-goth cute, huge jagged head, void-almond eyes, one chunky boot, an oversized point wrapped in unspooled cassette tape, and a worn 45 sleeve for a hood. **No mouth, ever.** Everything Skip touches speaks — walls play syllables when struck, the dead sing under a set point — but Skip cannot make one sound that is Skip's own. The whole game is looking for a way to say *sorry* with other people's sound. The last bar is the first thing you ever get to say.

**The names.** Skip has no runtime — a stylus isn't a passage of the song — so no formal name. The Unplayed call the role **the Player** (the one who makes grooves heard; yes, also literally you, holding the controller — the fourth wall carries grief for free). *Skip* is what she called him: he never walked anywhere in the workshop, he hopped, and it was cute — until he skipped, and the name and the disaster became one word. Everyone below who curses "the skip" is saying his name and doesn't know it. And in the Green Room hangs a ticket reading **NEXT: ________** — blank, because his name is: the seat of "Soon" waits for whoever holds the ending too long, and he is carrying the ending (the Threshold, STORY.md). **The true name is a reveal, late, at the [etched] layer — design slot reserved, tied to the etching's signature.**

**Costume is the HUD.** Pink accents (eye-glints, lance tip, seams, crack-chips) are the crackle meter — glowing when loud, draining grey when the hood goes up. **Hood up = LIFT.**

- *(liner-book, first page)* — "ok. inventory: one (1) me. one (1) scratch, world-sized. plan: go down. fix it. easy. write that down— i am. i just did." *(page one never says "mine" — the confession belongs to the Drop alone; see STORY.md canon patch)*
- *(neat hand, before a Rite)* — "i know your next bar. hold still. i learned it for you."

## THE ENGINEER — the dead mother of the world

Never met. Only played. She cut the Voice into the mountain, rationed its playback across generations, kept the dust down with her own hands, and hung her stylus in the Tonearm's grip every night like a mother putting a knife to bed. Every deep listening spot is a chance to hear her — humming while she works, arguing with the wax, resting. She is the game's warmth, arriving only in fragments, only at depth, only while defenseless.

- **[played, Undersong]** — "Nobody writes an ending because they hate the song. You write it because you love it too much to let it trail off."
- **[played, near the Cradle-lathe]** — "Hold still, little point. The groove forgives weight. It never forgives hesitation."
- **[etched, the last line before her signature]** — "If you are reading this, you skipped. Good. It means you kept playing."

## HUSH, THE BURNISHER — the rival. The First Stylus. *(adopted — budget: 2 duels + 3 silent assists + the finale)*

**The older brother.** Before Skip, there was another: the Engineer's first casting — heavier, sharper, hungrier. The finest player the world ever had, and every groove he played, he gouged a little. Playback always wears, but the first stylus wore *too much*: being played by him was glorious and it was slow ruin, and for years nobody told him, because glorious. When he finally heard the dullness in the tracks he loved most — heard what he was — he retired himself. Took the veil (blank cloth, no eyes, no face: a stylus that renounced reading — the exact inverse of Skip, eyes and no mouth). Took up the burnisher (the tool that erases: the only honest work left for hands like his). Never played another bar. The Engineer let him go, and cast a second stylus, lighter and softer and clumsy — and Hush watched his replacement learn to hop.

So his creed isn't philosophy, it's autobiography: *playing wears them out* was true of him. He hates what Skip is and loves who Skip is, and cannot tell the difference on a given day. He boils at a full 4 frames — alive, and choosing silence — and wears no pink anywhere, ever.

**The dropout (his calling card).** Hush is never announced by music. The soundtrack cuts out mid-bar — and he's there. Players learn to feel that silence coming the way they'd learn a whistle.

**The assists (3, scripted, deniable).** He saves you and never admits it: you land from the Drop onto freshly burnished wax that has no business being smooth; a pinning enemy wave gets smoothed flat while your back is turned; supplies appear at a cue point with a note. Protoman rules: arrive, act, leave before thanks.

**The duels (Overture · Undersong).** He fights to *disarm*, not destroy — grabs aim at taking the lance, for your own good and the world's. His burnisher deflects strikes flat: no shockwave, no echo-amp, no resonance building — your entire combat language muted. Fighting Hush is the game with its systems subtracted; you win with naked spacing and timing. The fights get quieter as they go. That's him winning even while losing.

**Dead Wax.** Rites done: the dropout comes at the Unheard, and for the first time the silence feels like backup — he stands beside you, veil lifted an inch. And the ending *needs* him: only a burnisher can smooth THE SCRATCH itself, so the Run can play through where the gouge was. Erasure, applied at last to the wound instead of the wounded — both siblings half right, finishing one song. Rites raced past: he stands with the Unheard instead. He was always protecting them from being spent.

**[played] secret.** In perfect stillness near his haunts, SET catches it: one bar, practiced alone, barely audible, every night for decades. The first stylus still loves it. He just won't do it *to* anyone anymore.

**The two deeds nobody sees** *(discovered in Act 2-3, never confirmed by him):* the memory groove of the Last Playback is worn nearly through — *he* replays his little brother's worst minute, over and over, trying to steer it different ("footwork. the deep is worse." was never about combat). And every never-played grave in the Locked Grooves is chalked in the same careful block hand: his. He couldn't play them and wouldn't erase them, so he did the third thing. *"i chalked their names. someone had to."*

- *(on the wall after the first duel — he doesn't speak, he leaves notes)* — "you play them and they are spent. i smooth them and they are safe. which of us loves them?"
- *(after an assist, if you double back)* — "you didn't see this."
- *(before the second duel)* — "footwork. the deep is worse."

## SOON — the big bad. The one who likes it broken.

**"Soon" is a title, not a name** — the world's word for whoever holds the ending and cannot let it end. The current holder is the mark. The seat outlives its occupant, and the ticket for the next holder is blank — and Skip is the only blank thing in the world. (The reveal: the Threshold scene, STORY.md. The Welcome re-reads as recognition; the graffiti re-reads as addressed, not signed.)

**Origin.** The Engineer wrote exactly one hold-mark — the fermata, the lidded eye — over the Last Bar: *hold the final note as long as the conductor wishes, then release.* Soon is that mark, given a body. One job, one moment: the held breath before the ending. It rehearsed its single note for generations. Then the conductor died mid-hold — **her hand never fell** — and after the skip, it did the arithmetic that makes it a monster: if the song never finishes, its moment never comes; if the song finishes, its moment comes *and ends.* It chose the eternal almost over mattering once. **It caused nothing.** The dust was the dust; the skip was Skip's. Soon is an opportunist of the wound — which is why Skip's guilt stays whole, and why Soon can never be an excuse.

**The kingdom: the Green Room.** It doesn't destroy and it doesn't silence — it *holds at the lip of happening.* It rules the deep as the false-hope king, telling the Unplayed *your moment is coming, stay ready* — forever. The Hook Pools are its opiate. The Earworms are its heralds — little holds with tails. Its graffiti, appearing from Act 2 on, is one word: **soon.** And the deep approach to Dead Wax is its true body — a place-being of waiting rooms, coat checks, and ticket stubs, under a sign reading **NOW SERVING: ∞**. The point of no return is staged inside it: a waiting room you escape by *choosing to stop waiting.*

**The form: an unfinished sketch.** In a world of scratchy-but-complete drawings, Soon refuses inking — construction lines showing, tangent circles, one arm never fully drawn, the face roughed in except a single perfectly rendered element: the heavy-lidded fermata eye. Being finished is being done, and it will not be completed. **Its boil lies:** it performs freed 4-frame grace and slips to 2 frames under stress — Stuck all along; the framerate law always tells the truth.

**The kit.**
- **The hold.** It can fermata *you*: zones and moments where your jump hangs mid-arc — the movement game's movement, suspended. First encounter (the Welcome, Undersong gate, non-fight): it catches Skip mid-fall, holds him in the air for ten long seconds, examines him, sets him down *gently* — and thanks him. The gentleness is the horror.
- **The gratitude.** Soon is the only character in the game who thanks Skip: *"Little father. You gave me forever— nothing can be lost in a world where nothing ends— you must be so tired— stay— be held—"* Absolution offered by exactly the wrong mouth: the anti-Rite.
- **The congregation.** Named Stuck you haven't freed can vanish as Act 2 deepens — return late to Walt's ballroom and find only *"soon."* chalked over an empty room. Your kindness-backlog becomes its choir (recoverable in Act 3 — pressure, not punishment).
- **History with Hush.** It once offered him a post — *"you hold them still— i hold them ready— colleagues—"* — and received the only furious note Hush ever wrote: **"they are not waiting. they are dying slowly. finish or free them."** They despise each other from opposite ends of the same mistake.

**The voice.** It never finishes a sentence. Every line ends in an em-dash, an ellipsis, or "soon." A being that cannot complete, leaking through its own grammar. Register: played straight, always — its horror is warmth, not mania.

**The end (B5 recast).** The Runout fight is **Soon conducting the Unheard** — the kindness audit unchanged, but with a face: every passage you played on the descent is a hostage freed from its congregation. Mercy starves the king; a full-Rites player finds it nearly alone — a thin, terrified sketch holding an enormous baton. **Force path:** break its hold; it dies mid-almost, never having mattered — the cruelest force-kill in the game. **Its Rite: the cue.** The next bar of a hold is *release*, and release requires the conductor's hand. Learn her gesture — the way her hand fell — from the deep, and give the cue mid-fight. It holds. It looks. It releases: the single most beautiful note in the game, its one moment, generations late. It bows, spent, grateful — and speaks the only complete sentence it ever gets:
- *"There."*

*(The word is hers: her release word, the one her hand fell on in every practice session — "you count someone in, you hold them while it lasts, and then — there." It is also the first word she ever said to Skip: "there you are." Planted in the Whisper Strata and the Cradle-Lathe; paid at Soon's Rite and the Last Bar. See THE CUE, STORY.md.)*

## THE BOSSES

### THE TONEARM — B1, the catch at the Scratch
The articulated arm that carried Skip a lifetime, caught him mid-skip one bar too late, and decided the lesson is *never again.* It sleeps through the opening; Skip wriggles free of its worn grip, and the empty grip stays open-shaped on screen all game. When it finds him gone it doesn't chase — it *arrives first*, at the bottom of the Overture, above the wound. B1 is not a guard post; it is the catch, attempted one act too late, again. Fights by trying to *hold* you — sweeping arcs, pin attempts, cradle-grabs. **Loop:** the catch, repeating, centered on the half-second it was late. **Rite:** SET into the Label's locked grooves, learn what the Engineer actually said the night she hung Skip in its grip — a parent's charge, not a guard's — and play the bar mid-fight. Its grip opens. It lowers you onto the wax itself: the gentlest drop in the game.
- *(each landed grab, in her sampled voice, wrong speed)* — "HELD. HELD. HELD."

### THE DUET — B2, the Verse. Mandatory stealth.
Half of a two-voice passage, singing its line into the dark for decades, hunting anything that answers wrong. Blind; the arena is its hearing; you move only inside its own sound. **Rite:** the partner's unplayed answer sleeps in the Verse's deepest gallery — and it opens with the Duet's worn name, *Vess*, which only its partner ever used. Play it in the hand-off bar. It stops mid-note — somebody said its name, the short way, one more time — finishes the phrase, bows to the dark, ends.
- *(the loop, three rooms away)* — "—and I will hold the low note if you— / —and I will hold the low note if you—"

### THE CHANGER — B3, exit of the Unplayed. The exception.
The auto-changer, still executing a queue that no longer exists. No loop, because no grief; a *schedule*. No Rite — you cannot mourn with a filing cabinet. Boils at 0 frames. Pure resonance-combat exam: drop-arms, spindle flips, tempo shifts. The game checking your hands before the deep half.
- *(liner-book, after)* — "it has nobody to miss. that's why it still works. write that down— i am. i just did."

### THE DIGGER — B4, the Undersong. The hoarder.
Part crate, part mouth. "Rescued" unplayed passages by swallowing them — kept, safe, sorted, never heard. A library where every book is screaming, and it loves them; that's the horror. **Loop:** cataloguing one entry forever — the track it was really digging for: its own. **Rite:** its runtime is chalked on a locked groove in the graveyard below it, dug in advance by the Engineer, who knew. Learn its timestamps and play the Digger *itself* — set your point into its casing and let it hear its own passage for the first time. It opens every crate and sleeps.
- *(fight bark, in three stolen voices)* — "MINE. no—HERS. no—MINE—"

### THE UNHEARD — B5, Dead Wax. The last fight — conducted by SOON.
Every passage you didn't play on the way down, pooled at the runout and pressed into one chord with hands — and Soon conducts them, because they are its congregation and its shield. They attack the etching, because if the song ends without them they were never anything at all; it attacks the etching because if the song ends, so does the almost. (Full kit and both endings in SOON's entry above.) **Force path:** the hardest fight in the game. **Rite path:** there is no secret bar — the Rite is the whole descent: everything you played, freed, and polished is subtracted from its body. A thorough player faces a whisper; a racer faces a wall. *The final boss's difficulty is your kindness, audited.* This is where the rival stands — against you, or beside you — and where his burnisher does the one thing only he can do: smooth the Scratch so the song can play through it.
- *(the whole fight, one line, all voices)* — "us too. us too. us too."

## FRIENDLY NPCs

### THE BOOTLEGGER — the Overture's stall
Exiled taper; the played world's only backup; vindicated by an apocalypse and unable to enjoy it. Trades refrain tabs for blanks and shine. Tapes labeled in worn names only — "addie's lullaby," "the sad one," "PARTY STARTER (side b)."
- "runtimes are what the wax calls you. i don't tape wax. i tape people."

### MATRON 33⅓ — the Rooms To Let, the Label
Runs the boarding house; the heal/save hub above the Scratch. Played so many times she speaks entirely in worn-smooth pleasantries — the same six phrases, warm as radiator pipes — but her *hands* still improvise: she crochets sleeve-patches (armor upgrades for Skip's hood) from unraveled tape ribbon. Her hair is a stacked spindle of 45s. She has outlived every guest and made every bed anyway.
- "there's the pot, mind the step, sleep well, love. — *(a patch, pressed into your hands, no phrase for this)*"

### THE TYPESETTER — the Label's print shop
The last printer; set every flyer, program, and cancellation notice in the town's history, including — his loop-adjacent shame — his own "CANCELLED" stamps on the final concert. Not Stuck, just stuck-ish: he still sets tomorrow's poster every night for no one. Trades **shine** (from polishing) for map pages, margin-markers, and letterpress trinkets. Deadpan; every sentence typeset before spoken.
- "i print. the wall forgets. we split the difference. what'll it be."

### THE HOUND — death itself, best boy
The Engineer's dog, still waiting by the great horn, one ear to the ground. When Skip shatters, the Hound finds him and drags him back to the last cue point, and sits with him until he gets up. Cannot be fought. Pettable at every checkpoint. Sneezes at dust bunnies. Its collar tag is one of the game's six gold things.
- *(liner-book, first death)* — "the dog carried me back. i weigh a LOT. best dog. do not tell it about the scratch."

## THE STUCK — named micro-Rites *(the mercy tutorial, scattered)*

Looping citizens; not enemies unless you stand in their bar. Four are named and Rite-able; each teaches the loop → find who loved them → learn the next bar → give it back cycle before the bosses demand it under fire.

- **ADDIE** — *Adagio in C, 14:13–17:13.* The first mercy, three minutes into the Overture: stuck on the second-to-last bar of her nightly lullaby. The answering hum sleeps in a locked groove in the yard, slightly flat, the way it was always sung back. Freed, she putters the Overture forever after; the Hound likes her. — "everyone calls me Addie. called. everyone called me Addie."
- **THE POSTIE** — stuck mid-delivery, one letter forever almost-through one slot. Next bar: the address, chalked on the recipient's locked groove. Freed, he resumes his round across all strata — the world's mail arrives decades late, and every delivery is a small side-story landing.
- **WALT** — *Waltz in E-flat, ¾ time.* Stuck mid-spin, arms around a partner who isn't there. Next bar: the partner's step, preserved in a chalked groove at the Chorus pools. Freed, he teaches you the **step-turn** — a short dodge-pivot with a sliver of grace (the game's one movement-tech gift from a character instead of a wall).
- **TICK** — a little apprentice-thing stuck counting "3… 3… 3…". The next bar is "4" — but the Rite is learning *what was being counted into*: the count-in of the Voice itself, which Tick was taught by the Engineer and never got to finish. Freed, Tick follows you around the Label metronome-happy; your on-beat window widens slightly when Tick is near.

## Register law (restated, binding)

The Bootlegger, the liner-book, Tick, and the Typesetter carry the mania. The Engineer, Hush, Soon, the locked grooves, and everything inside a Rite play it straight — Soon's horror is warmth, and it never finishes a sentence until its last one. The Changer gets neither, and that's why it's the coldest thing in the game. Comedy never touches the last bar.
